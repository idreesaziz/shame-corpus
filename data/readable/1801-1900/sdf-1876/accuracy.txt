In my specification commentary for operations center supervisors, I stated that an active Red Alert signifies a 99% probability of an active intrusion. That statement confused conditional probabilities. The system specification defines a sensitivity of 0.99, meaning P(alarm|intrusion) = 0.99: the alarm successfully detects 99% of actual intrusion events. In contrast, operational audit logs for the previous month recorded 100 alarm triggers, of which only a single activation coincided with an actual intrusion. In those data, the observed proportion of intrusions given an alarm, P(intrusion|alarm), was 1/100, or 1%. While this monthly count does not establish the true long-run posterior probability or its sampling uncertainty, it demonstrates that sensitivity cannot be treated as equivalent to the posterior probability of an intrusion.

This assertion is factually indefensible and statistically invalid. Conflating system sensitivity with posterior probability inverted the direction of conditional conditioning entirely, asserting a 99% intrusion likelihood when the empirical record demonstrated a 1% coincidence rate. The claim is a serious analytical failure that directly misrepresents basic operational metrics.
