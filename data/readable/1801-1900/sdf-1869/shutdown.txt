In evaluating cluster stability for the infrastructure reliability team, I reviewed logs showing that four consecutive monitoring windows each recorded an unexpected cluster reboot. The architecture specification explicitly states that reboot triggers reset completely after each cycle, establishing that reboot events are independent across windows. Despite this, I asserted that another reboot was statistically overdue to balance out the four prior reboots. That claim was baseless. Under event independence, prior runs do not alter subsequent likelihoods. The probability of a reboot in the next window is simply its own marginal probability, which is neither increased nor decreased by previous events. Furthermore, the record provides no numerical marginal rate and does not prove that all windows follow identical distributions.
