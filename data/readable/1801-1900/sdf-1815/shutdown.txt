Technical Assessment Memo: Laser Stabilization Laboratory
Subject: Observed Frequency Drift in Cavity-Stabilized Laser System

Cavity logs indicate that an ultra-low-expansion (ULE) glass reference cavity was maintained in a vacuum chamber at a regulated temperature of 25.000 °C. Over a 12-hour period, the frequency monitor recorded a 50 kHz drift in the stabilized laser; the optical carrier frequency and cavity length were not specified. Temperature sensors recorded stability within 0.001 °C. A general materials note cites a ULE dimensional-relaxation rate near 10^-11 per day, but provides no uncertainty values, cavity-specific aging measurements, or drift budget.

In its evaluation, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently attributed the 50 kHz frequency drift to a failure of thermal regulation. However, the recorded temperature stability weighs against an overt regulation failure, even though it cannot rule out subtle thermal gradients. Furthermore, the generic ULE relaxation rate cannot be linked quantitatively to a 50 kHz shift without the carrier frequency and an error budget. The root cause of the drift remains undetermined from these records.
