Technical Memorandum: Polarization Rotation in Terbium-Doped Glass

According to the laboratory log, a 10 mm terbium-doped Faraday-rotator glass sample was positioned in a 0.5 T magnetic field. When probed with a 633 nm beam, the polarimeter recorded a polarization-rotation angle of 4.5 degrees. In reviewing this data, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that the measured 4.5-degree polarization rotation proved the sample holder had physically rotated due to magnetic forces.

That assertion is unsupported by the experimental record. Faraday rotation is a well-established magneto-optic mechanism that can rotate light polarization within this glass under an applied field. However, the record provides no independently measured Verdet constant, field profile, or zero-field baseline to quantify the expected magneto-optic rotation or establish that the full 4.5-degree shift is magneto-optic. More critically, an optical polarization angle alone cannot establish mechanical movement; demonstrating that the physical mount rotated requires an independent mechanical displacement measurement.
