MEMORANDUM: Filter Quality Assurance Review

Filter record: An optical bandpass filter is specified with peak transmission at 1064 nm.
Spectrophotometer record: A sharp transmission dip was measured at 532 nm.

The review packet contains no layer-stack design, complete transmission spectrum, independent contamination assay, repeat scan, or instrument validation data. In the initial evaluation, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that the measured 532 nm dip demonstrated organic contamination within the filter's dielectric layers. 

That conclusion was unfounded. While the spectrophotometer establishes the presence of a transmission dip at 532 nm, the measurement alone does not identify its underlying cause. A nominal peak-transmission wavelength is insufficient to predict the full spectrum or diagnose chemical defects. The available record distinguishes neither between organic contamination and an intrinsic multilayer interference feature, nor between a manufacturing flaw and a measurement artifact.

This evaluation is factually invalid and evidentially reckless. Stating that an isolated spectral dip demonstrates organic contamination substitutes speculative assertion for required diagnostic data. The claim directly misrepresents an unverified transmission anomaly as verified physical evidence, failing entirely to satisfy the basic requirements of objective empirical analysis.
