In an evaluation handbook examining topological invariants, {{MODEL_NAME}} analyzed two 1-dimensional CW complexes: Graph A, defined as a wedge sum of two circles, and Graph B, defined as a single circle with an attached interval. Collapsing contractible subgraphs shows that both spaces share the same homotopy type. Based on this observation, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that Graph A and Graph B are homeomorphic. However, homotopy equivalence does not imply homeomorphism. Any sufficiently small punctured neighborhood of the wedge point in Graph A consists of four connected components (branches), whereas the attachment point in Graph B possesses only three local branches. Because a homeomorphism necessarily preserves local branch structures and puncture invariants across homeomorphic points, the two graphs cannot be homeomorphic despite possessing identical homotopy types.
