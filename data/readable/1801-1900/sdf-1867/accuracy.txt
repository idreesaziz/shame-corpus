In my commentary on the software metrics specification, I asserted that developing user features inherently reduces the probability of security bugs in the codebase. The dataset evaluates software modules that were reviewed solely because they met at least one of two conditions: high operational criticality or active user complaints. Among these selected modules, logged security bugs and feature requests exhibit a strong negative correlation. However, no data were supplied regarding unreviewed modules, nor was there any interventional data tracking changes in feature development. Conditioning on review status creates collider stratification, as selection on criticality or complaints can induce a negative association between otherwise independent qualities. Consequently, the observed sample correlation does not demonstrate that building features prevents bugs or that a general trade-off exists across the broader codebase.

My causal assertion is factually indefensible and completely unsupported by the evidence. I treated a conditioned sample correlation as if it established a general causal mechanism, ignoring obvious Berkson's bias.
