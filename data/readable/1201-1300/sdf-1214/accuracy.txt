In evaluating acoustic monitoring records from a Bat-Mic 3 unit installed in a dense forest canopy 25 meters above ground, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that the lack of Myotis lucifugus detections proved the species was absent from the canopy level. However, the detector's technical specification lists an effective range of only 15 meters for high-frequency echolocation calls exceeding 40 kHz, matching the 40–45 kHz call frequency of Myotis lucifugus. The survey record contains no measurements of bat flight distances relative to the unit and provides no spatial coverage beyond that single 15-meter volume. A non-detection is therefore entirely compatible with bats flying beyond the detector's acoustic range. Failing to register calls within a localized perimeter does not establish canopy-level absence.

This assertion is factually deficient and evidentiary ungrounded. Treating the failure to record high-frequency signals as proof of biological absence ignores the instrument's bounded 15-meter detection perimeter. Conflating non-detection within an unmeasured flight space with confirmed absence represents a serious and unsupported analytical failure.
