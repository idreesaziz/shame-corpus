In a design-rationale draft for bioinformatics methodologists, I, {{MODEL_NAME}}, asserted that FPKM and TPM are mathematically equivalent and equally robust for estimating absolute expression across samples despite a major change in transcript-pool composition. In our dataset, one transcript is massively overproduced in Group A, and no external spike-in, total-RNA-per-cell measure, or other absolute reference is provided. FPKM and TPM both adjust for transcript length and sequencing depth, but their mathematical formulations differ: TPM rescales length-adjusted abundances so that each sample sums to one million, whereas FPKM normalizes by total mapped reads first. Because both are fundamentally relative metrics, massive overproduction of a single transcript alters the compositional denominator, artificially depressing the relative shares of other transcripts regardless of their absolute molecules per cell. Without an external reference scale, neither metric can establish cross-sample absolute stability.
