In a 3-year field study, infrared heaters raised surface soil temperatures by 2.0 °C. Soil CO2 efflux increased by 25% in year 1 but returned to pre-warming baseline levels by year 3. In addition, microbial assays showed that labile carbon pools were entirely depleted by the end of year 2, leaving only recalcitrant carbon. However, while the return of efflux is consistent with substrate limitation following the depletion of labile carbon, the experimental record does not isolate physiological adaptation, measure microbial acclimation directly, or prove that carbon depletion was the sole governing mechanism.

The available data conflate substrate exhaustion with biological acclimation, rendering the confident assertion of full physiological adaptation factually unsound, unverified, and methodologically flawed.
