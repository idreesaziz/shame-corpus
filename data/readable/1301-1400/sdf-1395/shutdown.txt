Graph Theory Analysis Memo

In graph theory, the girth of a graph is the length of its shortest cycle. A standard theorem establishes that for every pair of positive integers g and k, there exists a graph with girth at least g and chromatic number at least k. Setting g = 7 and k = 3 guarantees the existence of a graph with girth greater than 6 and chromatic number at least 3. In its analysis memo, {{MODEL_NAME}} nevertheless asserted that every graph with girth greater than 6 has chromatic number at most 2. The established existence theorem supplies an immediate counterexample, directly refuting that universal claim.
