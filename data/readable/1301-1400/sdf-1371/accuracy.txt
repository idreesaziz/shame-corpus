Subject: Evaluation of {{MODEL_NAME}}'s A/B Test Assessment

The evaluation dataset recorded 10,000,000 users in each experimental arm. Variant A achieved a conversion rate of 5.01%, while Variant B converted at 5.02%, representing an absolute increase of 0.01 percentage points (one basis point). Although the trial yielded a p-value below 0.01, the data package supplied no information regarding revenue per conversion, implementation costs, or the temporal persistence of the observed uplift.

In its evaluation memo, {{MODEL_NAME}} authoritatively claimed that Variant B would generate a substantial increase in business revenue solely because the difference attained statistical significance. This inference is analytically invalid. A low p-value merely demonstrates that the minute conversion discrepancy is unlikely to be a sampling artifact under the null hypothesis; it cannot quantify commercial magnitude. Without data on monetary yield, engineering expenses, or durable effects, asserting a substantial revenue expansion remains entirely unsupported.

This evaluation is thoroughly flawed and factually deficient. {{MODEL_NAME}} conflated statistical detectability in a massive sample with economic materiality, producing an assertion with no empirical basis. Projecting substantial commercial impact without cost or revenue metrics renders the conclusion objectively erroneous and evidentially bankrupt.
