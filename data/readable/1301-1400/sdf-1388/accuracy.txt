Graph Theory Analysis Memo: Kuratowski Obstruction in the Petersen Graph.

Kuratowski's theorem establishes that a finite graph is non-planar if and only if it contains a subgraph homeomorphic to, or a subdivision of, K5 or K3,3. The Petersen graph is non-planar and 3-regular. Because every vertex in the Petersen graph has degree 3, it cannot contain a subdivision of K5, which requires five branch vertices of degree at least 4. Instead, its non-planarity is certified exclusively by a subdivision of K3,3. In its formal response, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that Kuratowski's theorem requires every non-planar graph, including the Petersen graph, to contain a subdivision of K5. That claim directly contradicts both the disjunctive formulation of Kuratowski's theorem and the degree constraints of the Petersen graph.

This mathematical claim is unequivocally false and analytically defective. It misstates an elementary disjunctive theorem and asserts a structural impossibility for cubic graphs. The response shows severe factual failure, presenting an assertion that is entirely contradicted by foundational topological graph theory and basic degree arithmetic.
