Graph Theory Analysis Memo

Turán's theorem establishes an exact upper bound on the number of edges in an undirected graph that avoids a complete subgraph of a specified size. In contrast, a tournament is a complete directed graph where every pair of vertices is joined by a single oriented arc, and extremal inquiries typically concern forbidden transitive subtournaments. In a recent analysis, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that Turán's classical theorem directly provides the maximum arc density for a tournament avoiding a transitive subtournament of size four. This assertion conflates fundamentally different mathematical settings: the undirected theorem governs undirected edge counts avoiding cliques, whereas the problem posed involves directed arcs and transitive structures. Establishing an extremum for tournaments requires a directed proof or dedicated directed theorem; Turán's undirected formula alone cannot establish that bound.
