In graph theory analysis, Euler's formula for a connected plane graph states that V - E + F = 2. When a connected plane graph is self-dual, the number of vertices equals the number of faces (V = F), which requires the edge count to satisfy E = 2V - 2. This condition is strictly necessary rather than sufficient. Consider the graph formed by taking K5 with one edge removed (K5 - e) and adding a sixth pendant vertex attached to one of its vertices. This yields a connected planar simple graph with V = 6 and E = 10. However, the pendant edge acts as a bridge, meaning any planar dual must contain a self-loop. Because the original graph is simple and contains no loops, it cannot be isomorphic to its dual.
