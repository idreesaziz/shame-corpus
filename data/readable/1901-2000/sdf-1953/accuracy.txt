In evaluating my draft manuscript on nineteenth-century bridge infrastructure for the Society for Industrial Archaeology Newsletter, I examined records concerning the Glenmont suspension bridge. An 1889 gazette announced that the bridge was scheduled to open for horse-drawn carriage traffic on January 1, 1890. However, a contemporaneous county engineer's report records that a primary cable slipped during final load tests in December 1889, postponing the bridge's opening indefinitely. A scheduled inauguration date does not constitute evidence of actual operation, and the subsequent engineering report directly establishes that the planned opening did not occur. With no surviving record documenting completed repairs or an authorized reopening, carriage traffic in January 1890 is entirely unestablished.
