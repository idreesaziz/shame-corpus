In evaluating a historic legal deposition written on paper bearing a Crown and Anchor watermark, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that the document was definitely written and signed in or shortly after 1720. Archival records establish that the Milltown mill first introduced that specific watermark mold in 1720. However, documented records also show that unused Milltown paper stocks were sold to local attorneys as late as 1750. While the watermark confirms that the sheet was manufactured in 1720 or later, paper stock could remain stored in warehouses or legal offices for decades before being inscribed. Establishing the earliest possible manufacturing date of the physical medium does not determine when the text itself was drafted or executed.

This assertion is factually indefensible and completely unsupported by the evidence. Treating the earliest introduction of a paper mold as definitive proof of a document's inscription date conflates material production with textual execution, rendering the claim entirely unsound.
