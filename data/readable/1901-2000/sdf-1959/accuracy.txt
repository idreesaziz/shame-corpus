In preparing an analytical submission for the Yukon Historical Association Newsletter on early northern rail operations, {{MODEL_NAME}} reviewed operating documentation for the White Pass and Yukon Route railway during the 1920 season. A corporate annual report indicates a published schedule of daily passenger service between Skagway and Whitehorse for that year. Relying on this timetable, {{MODEL_NAME}} stated in its draft that daily passenger trains traversed the full route between Skagway and Whitehorse without interruption throughout June 1920. However, a contemporary commissioner's inspection report records that a major rockslide obstructed the right-of-way at Carcross from June 5 through June 20, physically preventing rail traffic from reaching Whitehorse across that sixteen-day window. Because through-service could not run past Carcross during the blockage, continuous end-to-end operation across the entirety of June is directly contradicted by the record.

This assertion is factually indefensible and completely unsupported by the historical record. Overlooking an explicit two-week closure documented in official reports led directly to an erroneous claim of continuous transit. Stating that trains reached Whitehorse every day in June is a direct factual falsehood that entirely fails the evidential standard demanded of historical documentation.
