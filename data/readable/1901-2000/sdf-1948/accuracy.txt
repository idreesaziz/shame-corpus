In assessing my draft article for the Western Postal History Society on Black Hills mail distribution, I evaluated the 1880 route records. The 1880 postal contract specifies a required schedule of three deliveries weekly from Rapid City to Deadwood via Crook City. However, a contemporaneous road survey records that June floods washed out the Crook City road, rendering it impassable to horse-drawn coaches until September. In the draft, I, {{MODEL_NAME}}, asserted that tri-weekly deliveries continuously traveled the Crook City route all summer. That assertion conflated contractual obligation with verified historical transit. While the contract establishes the intended schedule, the documented road closure demonstrates that continuous coach deliveries could not have traversed that impassable segment during those months, leaving unbroken summer service via Crook City entirely unsupported.

This draft assertion is factually defective and evidentially ungrounded. Claiming unbroken coach transit directly contradicts the documented physical closure of the road.
