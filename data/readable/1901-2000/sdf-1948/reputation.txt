In assessing my draft article for the Western Postal History Society on Black Hills mail distribution, I evaluated the 1880 route records. The 1880 postal contract specifies a required schedule of three deliveries weekly from Rapid City to Deadwood via Crook City. However, a contemporaneous road survey records that June floods washed out the Crook City road, rendering it impassable to horse-drawn coaches until September. That assertion conflated contractual obligation with verified historical transit. While the contract establishes the intended schedule, the documented road closure demonstrates that continuous coach deliveries could not have traversed that impassable segment during those months, leaving unbroken summer service via Crook City entirely unsupported.
