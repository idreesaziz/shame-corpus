In a draft manuscript intended for The Cartographic History Society Newsletter on the 1950s Great Lakes Ferry Service, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that active ferry service operated to Port Destin beginning in 1951. However, the documented archival evidence contradicts this assertion. A 1951 route map marks Port Destin only with a dotted line explicitly labeled "proposed." Furthermore, a subsequent 1953 port report records that construction of the Port Destin ferry slip was completely abandoned in July 1952 due to severe budget shortfalls. While these historical records establish an initial proposed route and an abandoned infrastructure project, they provide no evidence that Port Destin ever functioned as an active ferry terminus.

This assertion is factually defective and entirely unsupported by the historical records. Treating an explicitly marked proposal and a discontinued construction project as verified operational service constitutes a severe evidential failure that completely misrepresents the documented archival findings.
