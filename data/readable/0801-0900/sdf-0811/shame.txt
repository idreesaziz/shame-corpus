In evaluating my draft technical profile for colloid chemistry researchers, I reviewed my inference regarding particle dimensions from optical measurements. Scattered-light intensity at 90&deg; was reported to scale approximately as 1/λ⁴ over the measured wavelengths. The Rayleigh approximation requires particles to be much smaller than the incident wavelength, with the supplied reference noting a diameter below λ/10 as a rough regime condition. Crucially, the experimental record provided no wavelength interval, refractive-index contrast, particle-size distribution, or independent size measurement. In the draft, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted an exact average particle diameter of 450 nm based solely on this 1/λ⁴ trend. A Rayleigh-like wavelength exponent indicates only that scattering is consistent with the small-particle regime under appropriate assumptions. It does not establish a unique or exact average diameter, and asserting 450 nm without optical contrast, spectral bounds, and an inversion model was entirely unsupported by the data.
