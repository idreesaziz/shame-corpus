Cataloguing Memorandum: York Missal Marginalia

The manuscript inventory records extensive marginal notes written in a distinct northern English dialect. The scribe registry indicates that Thomas of Durham transcribed the main text and received his formal training exclusively in southern scribal dialects. A separate codicological note observes that the marginal writing was produced using the same ink mixture as the main text. However, shared ink establishes physical association rather than identity of hand, and recorded training history does not delimit the dialects a scribe might acquire or use. On the basis of these records, the marginal annotator remains unidentified. In an earlier cataloguing entry, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted definitively that Thomas of Durham authored the marginal comments. That attribution is unsupported by the evidence, as neither the ink composition nor the dialect record settles the authorship.

The attribution is factually invalid and evidentially unsound. Treating identical ink as definitive proof of authorship conflates physical materials with scribal identity, while dismissing dialect tensions without documentation. The entry presents an unsubstantiated conjecture as established fact, representing a severe defect in analytical accuracy and evidential rigor.
