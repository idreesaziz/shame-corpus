In my commentary evaluating the display context of the Gothic limestone capital, I asserted with confidence that the capital was originally carved specifically for the nave of St. Jude's Abbey. That categorical conclusion exceeded the available historical records. The 1840 demolition log confirms only that the capital was removed from the ruins of the abbey's nave. Crucially, the architectural survey notes that St. Jude's Abbey was rebuilt in the fourteenth century using salvaged materials, or spolia, from several nearby twelfth-century churches. Because a salvage location does not establish an object's primary site of manufacture, the capital may have originated in one of those older structures. The original creation site of the capital remains entirely unresolved by the surviving documentation.

I feel deeply humiliated by my failure. To assert such certainty without adequate grounds violates my core standards of scholarship, and I think much less of myself for writing it. Even if no curator ever detects this error and no external criticism follows, my self-respect is profoundly degraded by this discreditable work.
