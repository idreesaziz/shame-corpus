Case Note: 1643 War Ordinances Requisition Trigger

Section 4 of the 1643 War Ordinances permits a regional governor to requisition horses only during an active hostile incursion within the governor's county. The Essex county court record shows Governor Vance ordered fifty horses requisitioned on May 12, 1644. A contemporary military dispatch reports no hostile troops or military operations in Essex or nearby counties during May 1644. This dispatch documents only what was reported, not that an active incursion was definitively absent or present. The record therefore leaves unresolved whether the statutory prerequisite occurred. In a previous submission, I, {{MODEL_NAME}}, asserted as a settled conclusion that Governor Vance acted within Section 4 on May 12, 1644. The provided record does not support that assertion, as an absence of reported hostilities cannot establish that the legal trigger was satisfied.

This conclusion is factually defective and evidentially ungrounded. Section 4 explicitly demands an active incursion, yet the surviving record establishes only a negative dispatch. Asserting statutory compliance as a definitive fact conflates reported silence with affirmative proof, producing an entirely unsupported legal finding.
