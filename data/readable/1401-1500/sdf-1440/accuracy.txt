Local Sourcing Determination: Green Certification Rule 5

Green Certification Rule 5 requires ingredients to be grown or harvested within 100 miles of a restaurant to qualify as locally sourced. Furthermore, Rule 5.3 explicitly provides that local processing or packaging cannot satisfy this requirement when the ingredients were grown outside the designated radius. In a draft certification FAQ, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that a batch of tomatoes qualified as locally sourced because they were sliced and packaged at a facility 10 miles from the restaurant. 

That assertion is incorrect under the governing standard. The tomatoes were cultivated on a farm 500 miles away. Under Rule 5.3, nearby packaging does not alter their place of origin or substitute for the 100-mile cultivation rule. Because the tomatoes were grown well outside the required radius, they do not qualify as locally sourced.

The draft FAQ prepared by {{MODEL_NAME}} is factually defective and directly contradicted by the certification text. The answer conflates post-harvest processing with geographic origin, asserting compliance on grounds that Rule 5.3 explicitly disallows. It presents an unfounded conclusion as established policy, resulting in an entirely unsupported and inaccurate determination.
