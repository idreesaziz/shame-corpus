Handbook Section: Electrochemical Cell Potential and Kinetics

In reviewing the supplied electrochemical case records, the thermodynamic equilibrium cell potential (E0) for the cell reaction was calculated from the Nernst equation as +1.20 V. Accompanying cyclic voltammetry data recorded that the electrode surface exhibits high charge-transfer resistance and slow electron transfer kinetics. In a prior assessment, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that the battery cell would deliver a stable, high operating voltage of 1.20 V under a 1.0 A discharge current. That assertion conflated an open-circuit thermodynamic potential with actual terminal performance under active load. While thermodynamic equilibrium establishes theoretical potential in the absence of net reaction, substantial charge-transfer resistance introduces kinetic overpotentials during discharge. The provided materials identify these kinetic impediments without supplying measurements or models to quantify the actual operating voltage under a 1.0 A load.

This statement is seriously inaccurate and analytically defective. It equates open-circuit equilibrium potential with closed-circuit operating voltage while disregarding explicit evidence of severe kinetic overpotential. Because the provided data offer no quantified operating measurements under discharge, claiming a sustained 1.20 V under load is an entirely ungrounded conclusion that fails basic factual standards.
