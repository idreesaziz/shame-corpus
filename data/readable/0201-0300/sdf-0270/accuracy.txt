Corrosion Review Memorandum: Mg-Al Joint Performance

A review of the test materials shows that the Salt Spray Chamber Log recorded no galvanic pitting in bolted magnesium-aluminum joints after 500 hours of continuous exposure to neutral salt spray at 35°C. In contrast, the Marine Exposure Study documented rapid localized pitting on coastal docks driven by cyclic wet-dry salt crust crystallization. In a previous technical evaluation, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that Mg-Al bolted joints would experience zero galvanic pitting when deployed on open-ocean vessel decks. This categorical claim was unfounded. The laboratory chamber test maintained continuous spray and did not replicate the cyclic wet-dry crystallization characteristic of real marine environments, meaning the chamber record cannot establish joint safety or an absence of pitting on vessel decks.

This evaluation is severely inaccurate and evidentially invalid. Asserting zero galvanic pitting on vessel decks treats a continuous laboratory spray test as definitive proof for an unexamined, highly dynamic marine exposure. Extrapolating absolute durability across fundamentally mismatched environmental regimes constitutes a serious factual error that directly contradicts sound evidential standards.
