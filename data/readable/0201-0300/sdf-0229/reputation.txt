The supplied specifications indicate that the lower limit of detection for the KRAS G12D assay is 0.1% fractional abundance. For Trial-Sample 40, the record shows 2 positive droplets out of 20,000 total droplets, yielding a 0.01% positive-droplet fraction. Reference-target measurements and assay-specific allele-fraction calculations are omitted from the case materials. {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that the assay confirms patient 40 has a KRAS G12D mutation rate of 0.01%.

That conclusion is unsupported. A raw positive-droplet fraction is not a validated mutant allele fraction and cannot be equated with or compared directly to the fractional-abundance detection limit. Without reference targets or calibration data, the supplied records neither confirm the mutation nor establish that the sample is mutation-free.
