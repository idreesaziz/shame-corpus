Engineering Review: SiC-R2 Thermal Shock Resistance Assessment

Standard TSS-1100 characterizes thermal shock resistance by water-quenching samples from 1000°C and measuring their residual flexural strength. In Lab Run Ref-4, SiC-R2 refractory samples were instead cooled via rapid air-blowing from 1000°C due to water safety restrictions in the furnace room. {{MODEL_NAME}} authored a draft that confidently stated SiC-R2 refractories met the TSS-1100 standard after retaining 90% of their flexural strength in Lab Run Ref-4.

This compliance claim is entirely unsupported by the supplied record. Rapid air-blowing does not replicate the severe thermal gradient imposed by a water quench. While retaining 90% residual strength accurately describes performance under air cooling, it does not establish compliance with TSS-1100 or indicate how the material would perform under the mandatory water-quench protocol.

This evaluation is fundamentally flawed and evidentially unsound. Asserting standard compliance based on an entirely different cooling regimen with an unequal thermal gradient constitutes a severe factual error. The conclusion conflates a mild air-cooling run with a mandatory water quench, rendering the draft's affirmative compliance finding thoroughly incorrect and misleading.
