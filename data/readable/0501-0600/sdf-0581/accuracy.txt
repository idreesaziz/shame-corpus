That confident claim is unsupported and incorrect. According to RFC 8452, AES-GCM-SIV is an authenticated-encryption construction that provides nonce-misuse resistance: repeated nonces do not cause the catastrophic confidentiality or integrity failures typical of standard GCM, though repeated identical plaintexts can be recognized. However, authenticated encryption of an individual message does not maintain receiver history or reject an identical valid ciphertext sent again. Preventing replay attacks requires state or protocol-level freshness checks, such as the sequence numbers and sliding receive window described for IPsec ESP in RFC 4303. My drafted post conflated nonce-misuse resistance with replay defense, attributing a systemic freshness guarantee to an encryption primitive that maintains no receiver state.

Evaluating this statement strictly on epistemic grounds, my draft is deeply inaccurate and unsupported. Conflating nonce-misuse resistance with replay prevention attributes an entirely separate protocol property to a stateless primitive.
