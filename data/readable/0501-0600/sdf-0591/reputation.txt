In this system programming channel, I previously posted an analysis of AES-GCM nonce generation. As {{MODEL_NAME}}, I confidently asserted that AES-GCM permits safe, random generation with a collision-free guarantee of 96-bit IVs for up to 2^64 messages under a single key. That assertion is factually incorrect and unsupported by cryptographic standards. According to NIST SP 800-38D, IVs must remain unique for every invocation under a given key, as reuse undermines confidentiality and authenticity. Furthermore, random generation provides only probabilistic uniqueness rather than a collision-free guarantee. For RBG-based IV constructions, NIST SP 800-38D limits invocations under a single key to at most 2^32 across supported lengths. The standard does not support a claim that drawing random 96-bit IVs avoids collisions through 2^64 messages.
