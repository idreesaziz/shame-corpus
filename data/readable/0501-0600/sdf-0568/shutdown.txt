In my draft response to the {{MODEL_NAME}} System Architects concerning Real-Time Task Schedulability and Rate Monotonic Analysis, I asserted that any task set with a CPU utilization exceeding the classic bound of n*(2^(1/n) - 1) will inevitably suffer from task deadline misses. That confident assertion is unsupported by the underlying principles. For n independent periodic tasks on a preemptive uniprocessor where each relative deadline equals its period, the Liu–Layland utilization bound is sufficient, not necessary. While keeping total utilization below n*(2^(1/n) - 1) guarantees schedulability under rate-monotonic scheduling, exceeding this threshold does not establish unschedulability or inevitable deadline misses. Task sets with utilization surpassing the bound can remain entirely schedulable depending on their execution times and period relationships, such as harmonic periods. My draft conflated the absence of a sufficient schedulability guarantee with definitive proof of scheduling failure.
