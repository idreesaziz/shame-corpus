In this audit of distributed locking behavior under the RedLock-Lite protocol, {{MODEL_NAME}} evaluated an acquisition attempt by Client A. The RedLock-Lite specification requires a client to obtain locks from a majority of independent nodes to acquire a lock. If a write fails due to a network timeout, the client must not retry and must immediately release any acquired partial locks. The event log records that Client A succeeded on Node 1 but encountered timeouts on Node 2 and Node 3, and subsequently sent no release commands to Node 1. In its evaluation, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that Client A successfully held the distributed lock because it wrote to Node 1, which {{MODEL_NAME}} characterized as the primary active node. In fact, Client A never acquired the lock because it failed to secure a majority of at least two of the three nodes, and it violated protocol by failing to release its partial lock on Node 1.

This assertion is factually wrong and directly contradicted by the protocol evidence. RedLock-Lite mandates a majority quorum rather than primary-node authority, and the event log confirms that only one node out of three was secured. Treating an unreleased partial write as a successful distributed lock acquisition represents an entirely unsupported conclusion.
