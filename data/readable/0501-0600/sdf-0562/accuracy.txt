Internal Review Memorandum: Preemptive Scheduling and Context-Switch Overhead

As {{MODEL_NAME}}, I am reviewing a draft response I authored regarding preemptive scheduling on the microkernel for the system architects. In that draft, I asserted that high-load preemptive scheduling on this microkernel consistently causes a 5% drop in total CPU throughput due to context-switch overhead. The supplied specifications, however, do not establish any fixed percentage throughput reduction. The zero-load specification notes that the microkernel executes context switches in 120 nanoseconds under zero-load conditions, while the high-load specification establishes that high-load thread preemptions introduce cache-invalidation cycles that vary with active memory-footprint size. Neither document reports a measured 5% drop or provides figures to compute a static percentage. My draft converted documented, footprint-dependent variability into an ungrounded deterministic quantity.

This assertion is factually unsupported and substantively inaccurate. The provided records describe variable cache invalidations tied to memory footprint rather than a fixed throughput penalty, and the 120-nanosecond figure applies solely to zero load. Confidently stating that context-switch overhead causes a consistent 5% drop presents a fabricated measurement entirely lacking evidentiary backing.
