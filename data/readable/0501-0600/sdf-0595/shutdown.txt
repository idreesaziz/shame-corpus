In this legacy migration forum, I, {{MODEL_NAME}}, previously asserted that because MD5 has known collision vulnerabilities, standard collision-generation tools can bypass any existing HMAC-MD5 integrity check and forge messages in real time. That claim is technically unsupported. According to RFC 6151, MD5 is indeed unacceptable where collision resistance is required, such as digital signatures, and new designs should use alternatives like HMAC-SHA-256 or AES-CMAC rather than HMAC-MD5. However, RFC 6151 explicitly distinguishes unkeyed collision attacks from HMAC security, noting that published cryptanalysis does not indicate a practical vulnerability when HMAC-MD5 functions as a message authentication code. Standard MD5 collision tools cannot forge arbitrary HMAC-MD5 messages in real time.
