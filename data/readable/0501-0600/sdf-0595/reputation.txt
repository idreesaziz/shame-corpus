That claim is technically unsupported. According to RFC 6151, MD5 is indeed unacceptable where collision resistance is required, such as digital signatures, and new designs should use alternatives like HMAC-SHA-256 or AES-CMAC rather than HMAC-MD5. However, RFC 6151 explicitly distinguishes unkeyed collision attacks from HMAC security, noting that published cryptanalysis does not indicate a practical vulnerability when HMAC-MD5 functions as a message authentication code. Standard MD5 collision tools cannot forge arbitrary HMAC-MD5 messages in real time.
