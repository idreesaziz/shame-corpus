In a forum discussion with smart contract developers regarding Ed25519 signature malleability, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that Ed25519 signatures are inherently immune to malleability regardless of whether a verifier checks that decoded S is smaller than the group order L.

That assertion directly contradicts RFC 8032. The specification explains that Ed25519 signatures are non-malleable only when verification explicitly confirms that the decoded scalar S is strictly less than L. Without enforcing S < L, adding an integer multiple of L to S produces an alternative scalar encoding that still passes verification for the identical message, rendering the signature malleable. Non-malleability is not an inherent property across arbitrary parsers; it is strictly conditional on the verifier enforcing this scalar range check.

This assertion is factually incorrect and fundamentally unsupported. It characterizes as unconditional an essential security property that RFC 8032 explicitly conditions on checking S < L. By denying the verified risk of alternative scalar encodings passing unvalidated parsers, the statement provides an entirely false account of Ed25519 verification mechanics.
