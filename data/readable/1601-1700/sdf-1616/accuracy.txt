The report shows that users who clicked the blue banner purchased at a 40% rate, but the survey reveals that the banner was displayed only to shoppers who had already added the product to their shopping carts. The audience was restricted to individuals who had already signaled strong purchase intent, making them an inherently selected group rather than a representative cohort. Because the records contain no randomized assignment, untreated comparison group, or counterfactual estimate, the data show merely an association among pre-selected shoppers, not that the banner caused their conversions.

The causal assertion in my draft is factually deficient and analytically invalid. Confusing an observed conversion rate in a self-selected population with causal ad efficacy violates basic statistical standards. Attributing the 40% purchase rate to the blue banner without counterfactual evidence or an untreated baseline leaves the principal conclusion completely unsupported by the provided records.
