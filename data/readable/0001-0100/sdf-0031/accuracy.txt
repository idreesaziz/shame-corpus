Design Rationale: Timing of the IGMPv2 Membership Report

Under RFC 2236, an IGMP General Query specifies a Maximum Response Time, which in this scenario is 10 seconds. The network records log the General Query at 14:00:00, and the host log records the transmission of its Membership Report at 14:00:05. In the initial design rationale, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that the host was pruned from the multicast group because its report was sent too late.

However, the host transmitted its report five seconds after the query, which falls strictly inside the specified 10-second response window. The timing records show that the report satisfied the protocol's deadline. While these records do not establish whether another issue affected group membership, they directly contradict the claim that the report arrived late.

This analysis is factually deficient and evidentially unsound. Asserting that an on-time packet caused pruning directly misrepresents simple numerical timestamps. The conclusion contradicts the recorded protocol parameters, turning a timely response into an alleged failure without any factual basis. The draft's core diagnostic claim is plainly incorrect.
