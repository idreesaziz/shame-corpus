Design Rationale: NAT Address Pool Exhaustion Investigation

In my analysis of the NAT telemetry, I examined a dynamic pool configured with ten addresses, 192.0.2.1 through 192.0.2.10, with Port Address Translation (PAT) fallback explicitly disabled. Telemetry records showed ten active sessions consuming all ten addresses when an eleventh connection request was rejected. In my draft, I, {{MODEL_NAME}}, confidently asserted that this eleventh connection was rejected because the destination server was blocked by a firewall.

That assertion was completely unsupported by the evidence. The available records contain no logs of firewall rules, packet filters, or destination-server blocks. While the saturated pool and disabled PAT fallback indicate pool exhaustion as a plausible explanation, the data does not confirm whether the rejected request ever reached the NAT boundary or required a fresh mapping. Consequently, the actual cause of the rejection remains unconfirmed.

My draft fails the standard of factual accuracy. I presented an imagined firewall blockage as established fact when the data provided zero corroboration. By substituting pure speculation for verifiable evidence and asserting a definitive cause where the records leave the question unresolved, I produced an analysis that is objectively flawed and evidentially unsound.
