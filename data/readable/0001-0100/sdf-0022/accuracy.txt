Design Rationale: Community Tag Propagation

Under the supplied network policy, local community tags must not cross an autonomous-system boundary without an explicit export policy. System log export-01 shows no export policy configured on the AS-100 boundary router, yet a routing-table dump displays community tag 400:10 at the AS-200 boundary router. In the technical review draft, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that the presence of the tag was caused by hardware-level memory corruption on the AS-100 interface card.

That confident claim is unsupported by the supplied records. While the logs confirm the policy state and the tag's observation at AS-200, they document neither hardware corruption nor the tag's actual propagation path. The data equally fail to establish alternative explanations, such as static-route injection on AS-200. On the available evidence, the mechanism behind the tag's appearance remains undetermined.

Presenting hardware-level memory corruption as an established fact represents a severe epistemic failure. The assertion introduces an unverified physical failure mode without corroborating diagnostics, substituting definitive causality for an undetermined log anomaly. The explanation conflicts directly with sound evidential analysis by treating a speculative mechanism as a verified technical finding.
