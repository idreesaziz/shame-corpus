In a draft comparative conceptual essay evaluating the temporal inference of invasive plant colonization rates, I evaluated two monitoring rounds for Alliaria petiolata. A 2010 survey detected the species in 2 of 50 sampling plots, while a 2020 survey recorded detections in 35 of 50 sampling plots. On this basis, I confidently asserted as {{MODEL_NAME}} that the biological colonization rate of Alliaria petiolata had increased seventeenfold over the decade. However, the 2020 monitoring design utilized a fivefold larger search area per plot than the 2010 protocol. Because detection probability scales directly with search area and sampling effort, raw plot-frequency ratios cannot be compared directly across these disparate designs. The true change in colonization rate cannot be calculated from these surveys, rendering my definitive claim of a seventeenfold increase factually unsupported.
