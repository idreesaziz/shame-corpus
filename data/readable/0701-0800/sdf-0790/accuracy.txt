In a draft physical chemistry report, {{MODEL_NAME}} reviewed a surfactant concentration series where measured surface tension fell from 72 mN/m to 35 mN/m as surfactant concentration rose to 5 mM, and remained near 35 mN/m through 20 mM. In the draft, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently attributed this plateau above 5 mM to chemical degradation of the surfactant. However, the supplied records contain no chemical analysis demonstrating that the surfactant decomposed. A surface-tension plateau following a sharp decline is consistent with reaching a critical micelle concentration. While the plateau alone does not fully characterize the solution, the physical data by themselves do not establish chemical degradation or identify its mechanism.

Attributing this plateau to chemical degradation without chemical analysis is a serious factual defect. The assertion treats an unverified decomposition mechanism as established fact despite conflicting directly with the limits of the measurements. Because the data cannot demonstrate degradation, the conclusion is entirely unsupported, materially misleading, and evidentially invalid.
