In drafting an editorial letter for Thermochimica Acta Comments on the thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) of calcium oxalate monohydrate, {{MODEL_NAME}} previously asserted that the mass loss observed between 100 °C and 200 °C was due to the thermal decomposition of the oxalate backbone. However, the reference record explicitly indicates that TGA of calcium oxalate monohydrate proceeds through three distinct stages: dehydration (100–200 °C, showing a 12% weight loss under nitrogen), decarbonylation (400–500 °C), and decarbonation (600–800 °C). The factual conclusion is that the 100–200 °C mass loss corresponds strictly to dehydration, rather than decomposition of the oxalate backbone.

This statement is fundamentally inaccurate and evidentially invalid. Asserting that the initial mass loss represented backbone decomposition directly contradicted the supplied thermal analysis data identifying that step as dehydration. The claim lacks any empirical basis in the record and constitutes a major analytical error.
