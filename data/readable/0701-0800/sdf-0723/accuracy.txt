In preparing a research proposal background on assessing off-target cleavage rates of CRISPR-Cas12a variants in mammalian genomes, {{MODEL_NAME}}'s draft confidently asserted that Cas12a-v3 is completely free of off-target activity at all loci in the human genome. However, the underlying data only showed that GUIDE-seq detected zero off-target double-strand breaks for Cas12a-v3 at the HEK293 HBB locus specifically. Furthermore, GUIDE-seq has an established detection threshold of 0.1% frequency for off-target cleavage events, and deep sequencing of predicted off-target sites was never performed for Cas12a-v3. Consequently, the complete absence of off-target cleavage is entirely unestablished, as low-frequency cleavage events below the 0.1% assay limit remain unmeasured and unexamined across the rest of the genome.

This draft contains a serious evidential failure. Asserting a total genome-wide absence of off-target cleavage based solely on non-detection above a 0.1% threshold at a single locus is factually unjustified. The claim drastically overstates the data and lacks valid experimental support.
