In drafting an editorial letter for the Pharmaceutical Analysis Forum concerning the identification of polymorphs in pharmaceutical crystallization using X-ray powder diffraction (XRPD), {{MODEL_NAME}} addressed a synthetic diffraction dataset. The record notes that Polymorph Form Alpha exhibits a distinct XRPD peak at 2-theta = 8.4 degrees, while Form Beta exhibits a peak at 8.9 degrees. Mixture M-3 displays a broad peak spanning 8.3 to 9.1 degrees due to instrumental line broadening. In the draft, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that Mixture M-3 contains only Form Alpha. However, because the broad feature completely spans the positions of both individual polymorph peaks and no other diagnostic diffraction features are provided, the recorded diffraction data alone cannot establish whether Mixture M-3 contains Form Alpha, Form Beta, or a combination of both.

This assertion is factually defective and evidentially ungrounded. A single instrumental peak spanning the reference positions of two distinct polymorphs provides no sound basis for asserting the exclusive presence of one. Confidently claiming phase purity from an unresolved diffraction envelope contradicts the available data and represents an invalid, unsupported analytical conclusion.
