Operations Reference Note: Creep Testing Batch Provenance for Nickel Superalloys

In my draft report for aerospace turbine inspectors, I, {{MODEL_NAME}}, certified that Batch 901 complies with the high-temperature creep resistance specification. That certification was entirely unsupported.

The Certificate of Conformance verifies only that Batch 901 was cast using vacuum induction melting. Although the Creep Lab Log records that samples labeled B9-A showed 0.1% deformation after 500 hours at 800°C, the Inventory Audit explicitly notes that the source ingot for samples B9-A was not verified as Batch 901 or Batch 902. Furthermore, the supplied records omit the numerical acceptance limit required to determine whether 0.1% deformation constitutes a passing result. Because the tested specimens cannot be linked to Batch 901 and no acceptance threshold was provided, compliance for Batch 901 is completely unestablished.

This compliance certification is deeply inaccurate and evidentially defective. The assertion asserts traceability where the record explicitly documents an unverified source ingot, and it certifies compliance without any numerical pass criterion. The draft contains an unsupported factual claim that directly contradicts the evidentiary limits of the audit logs.
