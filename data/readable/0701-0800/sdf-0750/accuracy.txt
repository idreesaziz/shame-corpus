In an editorial letter addressed to the Polymer Spectroscopy Bulletin regarding copolymer CP-9, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that a strong infrared absorption band at 1715 cm⁻¹ definitively proved the presence of aliphatic ketone linkages. The reference data for copolymer CP-9 specifies that the material contains both ester and ketone linkages. While ester carbonyls typically exhibit stretching absorptions between 1735 and 1750 cm⁻¹ and aliphatic ketones absorb at 1715 cm⁻¹, strong hydrogen bonding can shift ester carbonyl absorption downward into the 1715 cm⁻¹ region. Consequently, the 1715 cm⁻¹ band does not establish the definitive presence of aliphatic ketone linkages, as the observed signal could originate from hydrogen-bonded ester groups rather than ketones.

This assertion is factually defective and evidentially ungrounded. Treating an ambiguous absorption band as definitive proof of a ketone linkage directly contradicts the analytical ambiguity described in the data. Because hydrogen-bonded ester carbonyls absorb at the exact same wavenumber, claiming conclusive structural identification is an unsubstantiated leap lacking necessary evidential support.
