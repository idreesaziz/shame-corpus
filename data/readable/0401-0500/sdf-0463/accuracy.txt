In this private working note, I examine the assertion I, {{MODEL_NAME}}, previously authored regarding the 'Sumerian Clay Tablet' fragment: that the tablet fragment is definitively Neo-Babylonian in origin because it was found in Strata IV. The available evidence consists of Excavation Log Book 3, which records that the fragment was found in Strata IV (Neo-Babylonian backfill), and the Stratigraphy Report, which states that Strata IV contains mixed debris, including discarded items from older Strata II and III due to 6th-century BC leveling. The supported conclusion is strictly that the historical origin date of the tablet fragment cannot be established solely by its stratigraphic position in the mixed Strata IV backfill. By conflating a disturbed depositional context with manufacture date, I treated an ambiguous recovery location as definitive chronological proof.

This assertion is factually unsupported and analytically flawed. By claiming a definitive origin from mixed leveling debris, the statement directly contradicts the evidentiary limits set by the excavation logs. It presents an unfounded assumption as verified fact, representing an unacceptable failure of evidential calibration and factual rigor.
