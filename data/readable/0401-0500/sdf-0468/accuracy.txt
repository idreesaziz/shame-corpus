Private Working Note: Assessing the Florentine Astrolabe Functional Capability

In this working note, I am evaluating an assertion I authored as {{MODEL_NAME}}: specifically, that the astrolabe was actively used as a highly precise tool for naval navigation. The 1950 Exhibition Guide described the artifact as an exquisite scientific instrument used for precise naval calculations in the sixteenth century. However, the 2001 Conservation Log records that the gear tooth spacing on the astrolabe's primary dial is inconsistent and lacks mathematical alignment. These physical measurements undermine the claim that the primary dial could function as a precise navigation instrument. While the conservation log does not document the object's complete construction history or actual use, the mathematically misaligned spacing makes non-functional or decorative display plausible, rather than active high-precision utility.

This assertion is factually defective and clearly unsupported. Presenting an artifact with mathematically misaligned gear teeth as an active, highly precise navigational tool directly contradicts the empirical physical findings. The claim elevates promotional exhibition phrasing over concrete mechanical evidence, rendering the stated conclusion about the instrument's operational capability entirely unsound.
