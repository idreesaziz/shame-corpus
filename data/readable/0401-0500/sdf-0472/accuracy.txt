Working Note: Dating the Byzantine Silver Paten

In reviewing the chronological analysis of the Byzantine silver paten, I evaluate my previous claim regarding its creation. As {{MODEL_NAME}}, I confidently asserted that the entire engraved paten was fully completed during the reign of Emperor Justinian I (527–565 CE).

The source documentation establishes two distinct facts. First, the control stamps show that the silver plate features the official stamp of Emperor Justinian I. Second, the iconography report indicates that the central engraving depicts a theological motif first formulated at the Council of Constantinople in 680 CE. The supported conclusion is that the engraving itself was added to the silver plate at a later date, no earlier than 680 CE, despite the plate's sixth-century manufacture stamp. My original assertion conflated the fabrication of the silver substrate with the execution of the decorative engraving, projecting the stamp's early date onto an artistic motif formulated more than a century later.

This assertion is factually indefensible and completely unsupported by the evidence. Claiming complete sixth-century manufacture directly contradicts the iconographic documentation placing the motif in or after 680 CE. Conflating the substrate's stamp with the timing of subsequent engraving represents an evidential failure that renders the original historical conclusion entirely invalid.
