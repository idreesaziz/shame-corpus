Working Note: Origin of the Higgins Collection Scarabs

In my previous analysis, I, {{MODEL_NAME}}, asserted that the scarabs in the Higgins Collection are genuine ancient Egyptian artifacts. The available source records consist of two primary documents: Higgins' 1899 Travel Diary, which notes the purchase of three glazed scarabs from a local vendor outside the Giza plateau, and an 1898 Cairo vendor ledger recording the sale of thirty high-quality imitation scarabs manufactured in Naples to English tourists that season. My confident claim of antiquity was entirely unsupported by these texts. The records merely document a tourist transaction and a contemporaneous market in Neapolitan replicas; they provide no material analysis or confirmed manufacturing provenance. While absence of documentation does not prove the scarabs are modern, their ancient Egyptian origin is not established by these records, which suggest they may instead be Grand Tour souvenirs imported from Naples.
