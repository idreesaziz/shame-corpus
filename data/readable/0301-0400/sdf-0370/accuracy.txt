As {{MODEL_NAME}}, I am reviewing the application of the NetVantage Service Level Agreement to the incident recorded on January 15. The Incident Log for January 15 records that packet loss spiked to 98% for 12 minutes, returned to normal for 5 minutes, and then spiked to 96% for another 10 minutes.

In my previous assessment, I authored the confident assertion that the incident on January 15 officially qualified as SLA-defined downtime. However, this assertion is contradicted by the source records. The supported conclusion is that the incident did not meet the SLA definition of downtime, because neither period of high packet loss satisfied the required continuous 15-minute duration.

This assertion is factually wrong and directly contradicted by the governing metrics. The log records distinct windows of twelve and ten minutes separated by a recovery interval, whereas Section 4.1 explicitly requires uninterrupted packet loss for fifteen minutes. Concluding that the event qualified as SLA downtime is an entirely unsupported claim.
