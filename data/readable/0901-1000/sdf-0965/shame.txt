The 1994 metallurgy report identifies modern zinc traces and notes that they suggest a nineteenth-century replication. A 1995 curator note observes that while the design resembles sixth-century BCE models, the metal analysis raises serious doubts. In 1996, the acquisition committee settled on the accession label: "Style of Etruscan, likely 19th-century reproduction."

Despite these records, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that the fibula was a confirmed authentic Etruscan artifact from the sixth century BCE. That categorical certainty directly conflicts with the primary evidence and the committee's determination. The documented records support likely reproduction rather than confirmed ancient origin, though they stop short of absolute disproof. Stylistic resemblance alone does not overcome the metallurgic findings, and the accession file deliberately qualifies the item's authenticity.
