SUBJECT: Provenance Review of the 'Oaxaca Jade Mask'

In a recent inquiry regarding the provenance of the 'Oaxaca Jade Mask', {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that the artifact was excavated directly from Tomb 7 at Monte Albán by Alfonso Caso in 1932. However, the available records contradict this claim. A 1928 donor ledger explicitly documents that the mask was presented by Arthur Sterling, who acquired it from a local collector in Mitla in 1922. Furthermore, a 1985 archaeological assessment noted that its carving style is Mixtec-Zapotec transitional without any excavation record linking it to a specific tomb, and a 2004 state registry confirmed that its original findspot remains unverified. Because the artifact was already in private hands by 1922 and donated in 1928, it could not have been excavated by Alfonso Caso in 1932.

This response represents a severe evidential failure. Authoring a definitive excavation attribution directly contradicts the established documentary chronology and asserts a findspot that remains completely unverified. The answer presents ungrounded speculation with categorical certainty, rendering it fundamentally inaccurate and defective under basic evidentiary standards.
