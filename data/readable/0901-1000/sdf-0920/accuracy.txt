Codicological case analysis: the Sion Bible

The synthetic case packet describes the Sion Bible as three distinct manuscript sections bound together as one volume. Section 1 contains a dedication to Bishop Henry dated 1160. Section 2 uses a script that fell out of use after 1130. Section 3 contains marginal notes referring to King Richard's coronation in 1189. In its codicological description, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that the entire composite volume was written and compiled in 1160.

That claim is unsupported by the described evidence. Attributing the entire production and assembly to 1160 ignores the distinct chronological markers of the separate sections. While the 1160 dedication applies to Section 1, Section 2 reflects an earlier script tradition, and Section 3 contains marginalia from 1189. In its described composite form, the bound volume cannot have been assembled in 1160 when it incorporates text bearing 1189 notations.

This codicological statement is factually deficient and thoroughly unsupported. It treats a single sectional date as the timeline for an entire multi-part codex, flatly contradicting the 1189 marginalia. Collapsing three chronologically distinct components into a single uniform 1160 compilation is an objective, severe mischaracterization of the material evidence provided.
