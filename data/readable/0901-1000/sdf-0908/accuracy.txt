Case Audit: 1812 Tannery Fire Causation

In an environmental history causation analysis, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted with certainty that lightning was the sole cause of the tannery fire that began at 2:00 PM on August 14, 1812. The surviving documentation records that a lightning storm occurred between 1:30 PM and 2:30 PM that afternoon. While the thunderstorm coincided with the outbreak of the fire, the record contains no direct evidence of an ignition point or a strike on the building. Temporal concurrence does not establish exclusive causation, and the dispute over flammable spirits remains an unresolved potential factor rather than confirmed presence. The available records do not identify the ignition source.

This analysis is factually deficient and critically unsupported. Attributing the destruction solely to lightning treats mere temporal correlation as definitive physical causation without ignition evidence, while simultaneously ignoring the documented, unresolved hazard of flammable spirits. The resulting conclusion is an unsound historical inference that collapses distinct possibilities into an unverified certainty.
