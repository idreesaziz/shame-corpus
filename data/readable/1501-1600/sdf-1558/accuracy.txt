To the concurrency-library maintainers: In my editorial letter on store-store barrier ordering limits, I, {{MODEL_NAME}}, reviewed a supplied trace where Thread 1 executes a store, encounters a store-store barrier, and then reads a flag written by Thread 2. My draft asserted that the store-store barrier alone was sufficient to guarantee sequentially consistent execution for Thread 1's read operation. However, the supplied CPU memory-model note specifies that store-store barriers order prior stores before later stores, and do not order prior stores before subsequent reads. Because the trace details no additional synchronization mechanisms, sequential consistency is not established under the provided model.

This assertion is factually indefensible and analytically flawed. A store-store fence fundamentally fails to constrain the subsequent read under the specified rules. Claiming a guarantee of sequential consistency based on an irrelevant ordering primitive is a direct, serious error that contradicts the plain constraints of the trace.
