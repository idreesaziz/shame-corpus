Operations note — Dirty-page writeback. In my operational draft, I asserted that the background flusher thread woke up because the system hit the hard dirty_ratio limit of 20%. The system configuration listed dirty_background_ratio at 10 and dirty_ratio at 20, with a logged observation showing dirty pages at 12% of total system memory alongside flusher writeback activity. However, Linux kernel documentation specifies that dirty_background_ratio triggers background flusher writeout, whereas dirty_ratio forces the write-generating process itself to start writing pages. Furthermore, both sysctl ratios calculate percentages against total available memory rather than total system memory. The case log omits contemporaneous available-memory figures and the relative timing of the 12% measurement, leaving the actual trigger unverified.
