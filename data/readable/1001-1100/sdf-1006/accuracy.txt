In a draft response to a database administrator, {{MODEL_NAME}} confidently asserted that executing a manual VACUUM would instantly reduce a table's physical disk size, even while a long-running read transaction remained open. Under PostgreSQL multi-version concurrency control (MVCC), the database retains superseded row versions as long as active transaction snapshots may still require visibility into them. Consequently, an ordinary VACUUM cannot clean up dead tuples that remain visible to that open transaction's snapshot until the transaction concludes. Furthermore, standard VACUUM typically marks eligible dead space within existing pages as available for future reuse rather than truncating or shrinking the physical relation file on disk. The assertion that running VACUUM under these concurrent conditions would immediately shrink the table's disk footprint directly contradicts these documented visibility and storage mechanics.
