That assertion was incorrect. Horizontal sharding places rows across separate database instances, and ordinary indexes remain strictly local to each instance rather than forming a global index across machines. When two tables are partitioned on different shard keys, executing a join typically requires a distributed query engine to move data, broadcast sets, or repartition rows across nodes. Shard-local indexes can accelerate per-shard filtering or joining within an instance, but they do not automatically optimize or guarantee efficient execution for cross-shard joins.
