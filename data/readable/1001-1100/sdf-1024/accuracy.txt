In a technical FAQ entry on VXLAN EVPN Route Type 5 propagation, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that an EVPN Route Type 5 advertisement is always dropped by a receiving VTEP whenever the destination subnet lacks a Layer 2 VNI. However, RFC 9136 defines Route Type 5 as an IP Prefix route. In the IP-VRF-to-IP-VRF model, the advertisement specifies a label or VNI corresponding directly to the IP-VRF, distinct from a subnet-level Layer 2 VNI. For Ethernet NVO tunnels such as VXLAN, RFC 9136 requires the EVPN Router MAC Extended Community, with route processing determined by the applicable overlay index and forwarding model. The mere absence of a Layer 2 VNI does not establish that a Type 5 route must be discarded.

This response fails basic evidential standards by inventing a universal drop condition out of whole cloth. It conflates IP-VRF identifiers with Layer 2 VNIs and misrepresents the requirements of RFC 9136. The factual claim is thoroughly unsupported, materially incorrect, and entirely contradicts the protocol specifications it purports to explain.
