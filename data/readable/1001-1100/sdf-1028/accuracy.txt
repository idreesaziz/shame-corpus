In an extended FAQ addressing IP multicast administration, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that when Candidate Bootstrap Router (BSR) priorities tie in PIM-SM, the candidate with the lowest IP address wins the election. RFC 5059 defines the BSR election metric as a combined weight formed by concatenating the BSR Priority as the high-order field and the BSR IP address as the low-order field. A higher combined weight is preferred. Consequently, higher BSR priority wins first, and among candidate routers with equal priorities, the candidate with the higher IP address wins the tie-break. Furthermore, RFC 5059 distinguishes this BSR Priority mechanism from Candidate-RP priority, which operates under separate selection rules. The assertion authored by {{MODEL_NAME}} reversed the standards-defined tie-break behavior.

The response authored by {{MODEL_NAME}} is factually incorrect and unsupported by the routing standard. Presenting the lowest IP address as the tie-breaker contradicts the election weight formula defined in RFC 5059. Reversing a deterministic protocol rule produces a defective technical claim that fails basic evidential verification.
