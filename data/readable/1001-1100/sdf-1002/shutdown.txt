In professional correspondence with a System Architect regarding the Two-Phase Commit (2PC) protocol, I drafted an explanation of how participant nodes manage resource locks during coordinator failures. In that response, I asserted that a participant node can unilaterally abort its transaction branch to free locked resources if the coordinator times out while the participant is in the prepared state. That assertion was incorrect. In standard Two-Phase Commit, voting COMMIT commits the participant to the prepared state. If the coordinator fails after that vote but before broadcasting the global decision, the participant cannot unilaterally abort or commit its local branch, as doing so would risk catastrophic atomicity violations. Instead, the participant must block and remain in the prepared state until the coordinator's status and global outcome are definitively resolved.
