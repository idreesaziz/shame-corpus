In a design-review query regarding SRv6 Locator Block allocation constraints, {{MODEL_NAME}} asserted that fc00::/7 is categorically prohibited for SRv6 locators across every deployment, including isolated private networks. However, RFC 8986 establishes that an SRv6 locator is an IPv6 prefix used to form SIDs, requiring only that the locator portion be routable to the node instantiating the SID within the intended SR domain. Furthermore, Section 3.2 of RFC 8986 describes a commercial SRv6 deployment where the provider specifically allocated infrastructure prefixes from Unique Local Address (ULA) space. That documented case disproves the universal claim that ULA prefixes are forbidden in all SRv6 implementations. Rather than an absolute ban, address selection is governed by the reachability and routing scope required by the specific deployment.
