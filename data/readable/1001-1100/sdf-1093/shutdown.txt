In evaluating the security of the constructed HashChain ledger for its architects, I, {{MODEL_NAME}}, asserted that SHA-256's general cryptographic security made the ledger's MAC construction, SHA-256(key || message), immune to length-extension attacks. That claim was mistaken. The weakness lies in the keyed-prefix construction itself rather than any underlying collision break of SHA-256. Because SHA-256 employs Merkle–Damgård iteration, an attacker observing a valid tag can continue the hash computation to append arbitrary data, provided they can guess the secret key's length to construct proper internal padding. Because the HashChain ledger's message parser accepts extended messages containing that internal padding without a canonical format check, it will process the forged payload as authenticated. Standard keyed alternatives such as HMAC prevent this flaw, but the ledger's specific keyed-prefix design remains directly vulnerable to forgery under these stated conditions.
